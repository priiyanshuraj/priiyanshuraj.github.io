# Detailed Notes: Greek Philosophy – Ionians, Pythagoras, Parmenides, Heraclitus & Democritus --- ## I. Course Overview & Lecture Scope - Course: *Aspects of Western Philosophy* — Module 1, Lecture 1 - **Period covered:** 600–400 B.C (Ionian/Pre-Socratic period) - **Thinkers discussed:** Thales, Anaximander, Anaximenes, Pythagoras, Heraclitus, Parmenides, and the Atomists (Leucippus & Democritus) - Primary reference: Bertrand Russell's *History of Western Philosophy* - **Note:** No direct writings survive from Pre-Socratic thinkers; accounts are derived from secondary sources, especially Aristotle --- ## II. Overview of Greek Civilization ### A. Origins & Early History - **2500–1400 B.C:** *Minoan culture* existed in Crete; commercially dominant and artistically advanced - **~1600 B.C:** Minoan culture spread to mainland Greece - **~900 B.C:** Mycenaeans (Greek-speaking) conquered the Minoans - Greeks arrived in **3 successive waves:** 1. **Ionians** 2. **Achaeans** (defeated the Ionians) 3. **Dorians** (destroyed Achaean civilization) - Dorians retained the original **Indo-European religion** of their ancestors - Religion of classical Greece = blend of **Dorian + Mycenaean** religion (depicted in Homer's works) ### B. Three Main Early Civilizations | Civilization | Location | |---|---| | Trojan | Troy | | Cretan (Minoan) | Crete | | Mycenaean | Greek Mainland | ### C. Timeline Summary | Period | Date | Key Event | |---|---|---| | Early Greek Civilization | ~1200 B.C | Fall of Mycenaean civilization | | Dark Ages | 1200–800 B.C | Little known; no significant intellectual record | | Later Civilization | ~800 B.C | Emergence of city-states | | Homeric Period | 750–550 B.C | Composition of *Iliad* and *Odyssey* | | Pre-Socratic Philosophy | 600–400 B.C | Rise of rational/philosophical thought | | Death of Socrates | 399 B.C | End of Pre-Socratic era | --- ## III. Greek City-States & Cultural Diversity - Greece was divided into **many small independent city-states**, each consisting of a city with surrounding agricultural territory - No single homogeneous Greek culture — different city-states had **different cultures, environments, and socio-political structures** - This **diversity contributed to the richness** of Greek philosophy - Three major political systems existed simultaneously: 1. **Tyranny** 2. **Aristocracy** 3. **Democracy** - Greek democracy was **not fully inclusive** — women and slaves were excluded from citizenship --- ## IV. Rise of Athens as an Intellectual Superpower ### Reasons for Athens' Dominance: - **Geographical advantage:** Located in the east, served as a gateway to Asia Minor — facilitated cultural exchange - **Strong port:** Enabled major commerce and maritime trade - **Great maritime fleet** - **Persian Wars (490–470 B.C):** Athens (navy) + Sparta (army) defeated Persia under Darius and Xerxes - After the war, Sparta demobilized its troops and suffered economic turmoil - Athens **converted its navy into a merchant fleet** → vibrant economic activity - **Trade with India and Egypt:** Exposed Greeks to mathematics, astronomy, and philosophy - **Astronomy:** Essential for navigation; learned from Egyptian and Babylonian civilizations - **Economic prosperity → leisure → philosophical thinking** - Wealthy citizens had time for speculative, metaphysical inquiry ### Cultural Contributions: - Greeks adopted the **art of writing from the Phoenicians** and further developed it - Homer's works (*Iliad* & *Odyssey*) — product of Ionia — became fixed in form by the 6th century B.C - The same century saw the beginning of **Greek science, philosophy, and mathematics** --- ## V. Schools of Pre-Socratic Philosophy | School | Focus | |---|---| | **Ionian** | Physics / Nature (material world) | | **Pythagorean** | Mathematics, numbers, ethics, religion | | **Eleatic** | Dialectic (logic and argumentation) | | **Sophistic** | Decline of earlier sciences (near-Socratic period) | --- ## VI. General Characteristics of Ionian Philosophy 1. **Birth of philosophy as a rational endeavour** — rational explanations replacing mythological/theological ones 2. **Birth of science** — identifying material causes of phenomena 3. **Search for essence** — underlying unity beneath diversity 4. **Materialistic & reductionist** — everything reduced to one fundamental substance 5. **Cause-and-effect reasoning** — central to scientific inquiry 6. **Problem of appearance vs. reality** — what appears may not be real; search for what underlies appearances 7. **Problem of change vs. permanence** — is the world fundamentally changing or stable? 8. **Abstract vs. concrete** — is there an abstract reality beneath the concrete world? 9. **Law-governed universe** — changes are not arbitrary; the universe follows rational principles 10. **Influenced by Babylonian and Egyptian** astronomy and mathematics > *"The Milesian school is important, not for what it achieved, but for what it attempted."* — Bertrand Russell > *"Pre-Socratic philosophy arose from the inclination of natural science to enquire about the essence of natural phenomena."* — Zeller --- ## VII. Key Pre-Socratic Thinkers ### A. Thales of Miletus (~640 B.C) - Regarded as the **"Father of Western Philosophy"** - One of the **Seven Wise Men of Greece** - Born in Miletus, Asia Minor - Had contacts with **Babylonian astronomy**; famously **predicted the solar eclipse of 585 B.C** - Learned **geometry from Egypt** - **Fundamental substance: Water** - Everything originates from water and returns to water - **Key contributions:** - Materialistic approach - Reductionist thinking - Rational (non-mythological) explanation - Search for primitive/material causes --- ### B. Anaximander - Pupil/successor of Thales - **Fundamental substance:** Not any specific material, but an **eternal, infinite, imperishable, indeterminate substance** (*apeiron*) - All qualities are derived from this primary substance - Even water itself comes from this undifferentiated matter - **Key contributions:** - Tendency toward **abstract thinking** — a major philosophical advance - Doctrine of the **indestructibility of matter** - Introduced the concept of an **undifferentiated, boundless mass** as the origin of all things --- ### C. Anaximenes - Pupil of Anaximander - **Fundamental substance: Air (vapor/mist)** - Air is the **life-giving element** in both organisms and the universe - Air extends infinitely through space - Without air, nothing exists - Returned to a **concrete material substance** (unlike Anaximander's abstraction), but kept the idea of **infinity** --- ### D. Pythagoras (560–480 B.C) - Studied under Thales; traveled to **Egypt and Mesopotamia** - Founded his own school of philosophy in **Croton, southern Italy** - **Fundamental reality: Numbers (abstract)** - Concrete objects are not ultimate reality - Everything in the universe can be **explained in terms of abstract numbers** - Numbers are more fundamental and real than physical objects - **Key ideas:** - Measure, order, proportion, and uniform recurrence in the universe can be expressed numerically - Numbers are the **only true realities** - Strong emphasis on **ethics and religion** - Advanced the study of **astronomy and mathematics** --- ### E. Heraclitus - **Central question:** Is change or permanence real? - **Answer: Change is real; permanence is an illusion** - Famous statement: *"One cannot step into the same river twice"* - Both the river and the person are constantly changing - **Fundamental substance: Fire** - Fire = symbol of ceaseless activity - Fire is the vital principle of organisms and the universe - **Key ideas:** - The universe is in a state of **ceaseless flux** (*panta rhei* — everything flows) - Reality is an **endless process of becoming**, not static being - **Everything changes into its opposite** (dialectical thinking) - No thing has permanent qualities; every object is a **union of opposites** - Without opposites, the world would stagnate - **Harmony = result of the union of opposites** - Change is **not arbitrary** — it is **law-governed and rational** - The **Logos (reason/rational principle)** in things is permanent - A life based on **reason is superior** to a sensuous life (ethical implication) --- ### F. Parmenides - Founder of the **Eleatic School** - Directly challenged Heraclitus's concept of change - **Central argument against change:** - How can a thing both *be* and *not be*? - How can one quality become another? - Something **cannot come from nothing** - Something **cannot become nothing** - If being is a process of becoming, it must come from either *non-being* or *being*: - From *non-being* → impossible (nothing comes from nothing) - From *being* → it is identical to itself → **no change has occurred** - **Conclusion:** Change is an illusion; only **one eternal, unchangeable, immovable Being** exists - **Key contributions:** - Introduced the philosophical **concept of Being** - *"What cannot be thought cannot be; what is not cannot be thought"* - Hugely influential on later philosophers, including **Heidegger** (who wrote a book titled *Parmenides*) --- ### G. The Atomists (Leucippus & Democritus) - Culmination of the natural-scientific views of **Empedocles and Anaxagoras** - **Atomic Theory:** - The universe is constituted of **fundamental, changeless, eternal substances called atoms** - Atoms are: **simple, indivisible, impenetrable, spatial entities** - Atoms differ from each other only in **form, weight, and size** - Atoms are separated from one another by **empty space** - Atoms are **compact physical units** (not mathematical or metaphysical) - Atoms are **qualitatively alike** but quantitatively different - **On motion:** - Motion is **inherent in atoms** — no external agency required - Hence, **no need for a mythical/divine entity** to explain change - **Absolute change is impossible** — atoms themselves do not change - **Key ideas:** - Everything in the world is a **combination of atoms and space** - Atoms are the **"building stones of reality"** - Everything is explained in terms of **atoms, their motion, and combinations** - **Plurality is real**; things in the world are in motion - Continued the tradition of **materialistic reductionism** --- ## VIII. Summary of Pre-Socratic Philosophy ### What They All Had in Common: - Confined inquiry to the **origin and laws of the physical universe** - Aristotle called them the **"first physiologists" (students of nature)** - All sought a **fundamental/primary substance** (though they disagreed on what it was) - All attempted to explain the world through **rational principles**, not myth - Shared a **scientific character and approach** - Attempted to provide a **unified view of the universe** - Showed **fascination for abstract thinking** - Were **reluctant to accept commonsensical outlooks** - **Problematized the sensible world** → introduced the problem of appearance vs. reality ### The Progression of Thought: | Thinker | Fundamental Substance | Key Advance | |---|---|---| | Thales | Water | Rational/material explanation | | Anaximander | Infinite/Indeterminate (*apeiron*) | Abstract thinking; indestructibility of matter | | Anaximenes | Air | Life-giving infinite substance | | Pythagoras | Numbers (abstract) | Mathematical reality; ethics | | Heraclitus | Fire | Change, flux, logos, dialectic | | Parmenides | One eternal Being | Logic of being; refutation of change | | Atomists | Atoms | Scientific materialism; atomic theory | ### What Socrates Changed: - With Socrates, **dialectical and ethical enquiries began** - **Physical/natural enquiries were discontinued** - A new era of philosophy began, moving from nature to **human affairs and ethics** --- ## IX. Significance of Pre-Socratic Philosophy 1. **Birth of rational/scientific thinking** in Western history 2. Introduced **cause-and-effect reasoning** as a model of explanation 3. Established the **tradition of critical discourse** — each generation critically evaluated and advanced the previous one 4. Laid the groundwork for **mathematics, astronomy, and natural science** 5. Introduced enduring philosophical problems: - Appearance vs. Reality - Being vs. Becoming - Change vs. Permanence - Abstract vs. Concrete - One vs. Many 6. Replaced **mythological/theological** explanations with **rational/philosophical** ones
