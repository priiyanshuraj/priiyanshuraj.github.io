# II. Overview of Greek Civilization

## A. Origins & Early History

- **2500–1400 B.C:** *Minoan culture* existed in Crete; commercially dominant and artistically advanced  
- **~1600 B.C:** Minoan culture spread to mainland Greece  
- **~900 B.C:** Mycenaeans (Greek-speaking) conquered the Minoans  

Greeks arrived in **three successive waves:**

1. **Ionians**
2. **Achaeans** (defeated the Ionians)
3. **Dorians** (destroyed Achaean civilization)

- Dorians retained the original **Indo-European religion** of their ancestors.
- Religion of classical Greece = blend of **Dorian + Mycenaean religion** (depicted in Homer's works).

# III. Greek City-States & Cultural Diversity

- Greece was divided into **many small independent city-states**, each consisting of a city with surrounding agricultural territory.
- There was **no single homogeneous Greek culture**.
- Different city-states had **different cultures, environments, and socio-political structures**.
- This **diversity contributed to the richness** of Greek philosophy.

Three major political systems existed simultaneously:

1. **Tyranny**
2. **Aristocracy**
3. **Democracy**

Greek democracy was **not fully inclusive** — women and slaves were excluded from citizenship.

---

# IV. Rise of Athens as an Intellectual Superpower

## Reasons for Athens' Dominance

- **Geographical advantage:** Gateway to Asia Minor → cultural exchange
- **Strong port:** Enabled commerce and maritime trade
- **Powerful navy**
- **Persian Wars (490–470 B.C):** Athens (navy) + Sparta (army) defeated Persia under Darius and Xerxes

After the war:

- Sparta demobilized its troops and suffered economic turmoil.
- Athens **converted its navy into a merchant fleet**, leading to economic growth.

### Trade & Intellectual Exchange

Trade with **India and Egypt** exposed Greeks to:

- Mathematics
- Astronomy
- Philosophy

Astronomy was crucial for **navigation**, and Greeks learned it from **Egyptian and Babylonian civilizations**.

Economic prosperity → **leisure → philosophical speculation**.

### Cultural Contributions

- Greeks adopted **writing from the Phoenicians**.
- Homer's works (*Iliad* and *Odyssey*) originated in **Ionia**.
- By the **6th century B.C**, these works were standardized.

The same century saw the emergence of:

- **Greek science**
- **Greek philosophy**
- **Greek mathematics**

# VI. General Characteristics of Ionian Philosophy

1. Birth of **philosophy as rational inquiry**
2. Birth of **science**
3. Search for **underlying unity**
4. **Materialistic and reductionist thinking**
5. **Cause-and-effect reasoning**
6. Problem of **appearance vs reality**
7. Problem of **change vs permanence**
8. Debate over **abstract vs concrete reality**
9. Belief in a **law-governed universe**
10. Influence of **Babylonian and Egyptian astronomy**

> *"The Milesian school is important, not for what it achieved, but for what it attempted."*  
> — Bertrand Russell

> *"Pre-Socratic philosophy arose from the inclination of natural science to enquire about the essence of natural phenomena."*

# VII. Key Pre-Socratic Thinkers

---

## A. Thales of Miletus (~640 B.C)

- Regarded as the **Father of Western Philosophy**
- One of the **Seven Wise Men of Greece**
- Born in **Miletus (Asia Minor)**

### Influences

- Babylonian astronomy
- Egyptian geometry

Famously **predicted the solar eclipse of 585 B.C**.

### Fundamental Substance

**Water**

Everything originates from water and returns to water.

### Contributions

- Materialistic explanation of reality
- Reductionist thinking
- Rational explanations replacing myth

---

## B. Anaximander

- Student of **Thales**

### Fundamental Substance

**Apeiron** — an infinite, indeterminate substance.

Key ideas:

- Origin of all things is **undifferentiated matter**
- **Indestructibility of matter**
- Shift toward **abstract philosophical thinking**

---

## C. Anaximenes

- Student of **Anaximander**

### Fundamental Substance

**Air**

Air is:

- Life-giving
- Infinite
- Present everywhere

Returned to a **concrete substance** but preserved the idea of **infinity**.

---

## D. Pythagoras (560–480 B.C)

- Studied under **Thales**
- Traveled to **Egypt and Mesopotamia**
- Founded a philosophical school in **Croton (Southern Italy)**

### Fundamental Reality

**Numbers**

Numbers are more fundamental than physical objects.

### Key Ideas

- Reality is **mathematical**
- Universe governed by **order and proportion**
- Strong emphasis on **ethics and religion**
- Major contributions to **mathematics and astronomy**

---

## E. Heraclitus

### Central Question

Is **change** or **permanence** real?

### Answer

**Change is real.**

Famous statement:

> "One cannot step into the same river twice."

### Fundamental Substance

**Fire**

Fire symbolizes **constant transformation**.

### Key Ideas

- **Panta rhei** — everything flows
- Reality is **continuous becoming**
- **Unity of opposites**
- Harmony arises from conflict
- Universe governed by **Logos (rational principle)**

---

## F. Parmenides

Founder of the **Eleatic School**.

### Argument Against Change

- Something **cannot come from nothing**
- Something **cannot become nothing**

Therefore:

**Change is impossible.**

### Conclusion

Reality is:

- One
- Eternal
- Unchanging
- Immovable

### Key Idea

> "What cannot be thought cannot be."

His ideas strongly influenced **Heidegger**.

---

## G. Atomists (Leucippus & Democritus)

Developed **Atomic Theory**.

### Atoms

Atoms are:

- Eternal
- Indivisible
- Solid
- Differ only in **shape, weight, and size**

Atoms move through **empty space**.

### Key Ideas

- Everything consists of **atoms + void**
- Motion is **inherent**
- No divine explanation required

Reality = **atoms in motion**.

# VIII. Summary of Pre-Socratic Philosophy

## Shared Characteristics

All thinkers:

- Investigated **origin of the universe**
- Sought a **fundamental substance**
- Used **rational explanations**
- Avoided mythological explanations
- Developed early **scientific thinking**

Aristotle called them:

**"The first physiologists."**

| Thinker | Fundamental Substance | Contribution |
|---|---|---|
| Thales | Water | Rational explanation |
| Anaximander | Apeiron | Abstract thinking |
| Anaximenes | Air | Infinite substance |
| Pythagoras | Numbers | Mathematical reality |
| Heraclitus | Fire | Flux and Logos |
| Parmenides | Being | Logic of being |
| Atomists | Atoms | Scientific materialism |

# IX. Significance of Pre-Socratic Philosophy

1. Birth of **rational thinking**
2. Development of **cause-and-effect reasoning**
3. Beginning of **critical philosophical debate**
4. Foundations of **science and mathematics**
5. Introduction of enduring philosophical problems:

- Appearance vs Reality  
- Being vs Becoming  
- Change vs Permanence  
- Abstract vs Concrete  
- One vs Many  

6. Replacement of **mythological explanations** with **rational inquiry**.
